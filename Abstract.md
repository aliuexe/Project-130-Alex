# Abstract

Neoantigens — mutant peptides presented by HLA molecules — are central targets of cancer immunotherapy, but their identification requires the integration of several genomic data types. This project presents a reproducible computational pipeline that links somatic mutation, gene-expression, and HLA presentation data to nominate candidate neoantigens in colorectal cancer. Somatic mutation data for the TCGA-COAD cohort were obtained from the NCI Genomic Data Commons as a project-level Masked Somatic Mutation file (GRCh38) and filtered to protein-coding, high-confidence, missense single-nucleotide variants, yielding 153,996 distinct mutations across 586 tumour samples, represented as a binary mutation-by-sample matrix. Matched TCGA colorectal RNA-seq data (cBioPortal) were converted from RSEM to TPM by per-sample rescaling, producing a gene-by-sample expression matrix of 20,511 genes across 592 samples; the two datasets were then integrated using the median TPM per gene as a cancer-level expression summary. For the advanced component, 145,612 missense mutations were mapped to their VEP-canonical transcripts and translated into all mutation-containing 9-mer and 15-mer peptides together with their wild-type counterparts (6,873,140 peptides). Mutant and wild-type 9-mers were scored against a fixed panel of three common HLA class I alleles using BigMHC (Albert et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2023), evaluating eluted-ligand presentation probability (`BigMHC_EL`) and T-cell immunogenicity (`BigMHC_IM`). Candidates were prioritised according to presentation strength, stronger-than-wild-type presentation, gene expression, and per-mutation recurrence. A total of 30,260 strong-presenting mutant peptide–allele pairs were identified, alongside a prioritised candidate shortlist. The most recurrent candidates corresponded to the canonical colorectal driver neoantigens KRAS G12V/G12C/G12A, PIK3CA E542K, and SMAD4 R361H, with KRAS G12x recovered on HLA-A\*03:01. The resulting workflow provides an end-to-end, reproducible approach to neoantigen discovery whose predictions recapitulate the known colorectal neoantigen landscape, while remaining computational prioritisations rather than experimental validation.
