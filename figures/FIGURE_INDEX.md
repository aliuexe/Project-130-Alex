# Presentation figure index — Project 130 (TCGA-COAD neoantigens)

Thirteen figures in `figures/`, grouped by the five oral-presentation points
(Part III E). Each entry gives the file, a caption, and a talking point.

## 1. Dataset selection
- **fig6_variant_classification.png** — Somatic variant classes in the raw
  TCGA-COAD MAF; missense SNVs (red) are the substrate for the neoantigen
  analysis. *Talking point:* why we filter to protein-coding PASS missense
  SNVs on GRCh38.
- **fig1_top_mutated_genes.png** — Ten most frequently mutated genes.
  *Talking point:* large genes (TTN, MUC16) top the list, but the true
  colorectal drivers TP53, KRAS, PIK3CA are recovered at expected frequency —
  a sanity check that the cohort and filtering are correct.

## 2. Computational workflow
- **fig13_workflow_schematic.png** — The eight numbered, reproducible scripts
  from MAF + RNA-seq to prioritised neoantigens.
- **fig5_methods_funnel.png** — Quantitative funnel: 310,472 raw records →
  184,574 filtered missense SNVs → 6.87 M peptides → 2.46 M scored 9-mers →
  30,260 strong mutant binders → 1,536 prioritised candidates.
  *Talking point:* shows scale and where each stage narrows the search.

## 3. Major findings
- **fig2_genelevel_tpm_distribution.png** — Distribution of GeneLevelTPM
  (median tumour TPM) across mutated genes (log scale).
- **fig3_mutation_heatmap.png** — Presence/absence of the 30 most recurrent
  mutations across tumour samples.
- **fig4_freq_vs_expression.png** — Integration: mutation frequency vs gene
  expression per gene; drivers are both recurrent and expressed.
- **fig7_mut_vs_wt_affinity.png** — HLA-I binding distributions for mutant vs
  wild-type 9-mers, with 50 nM / 500 nM cutoffs.
- **fig9_binder_class_mut_vs_wt.png** — Strong / weak / non-binder counts,
  mutant vs wild-type.
- **fig10_strong_binders_by_allele.png** — Predicted binders per HLA allele;
  HLA-A*02:01 yields the most, then A*03:01, then A*01:01.

## 4. One technical challenge
- **fig8_delta_affinity.png** — DeltaAffinity (WT − Mutant); 54% of mutant
  9-mers bind more strongly than wild-type. *Talking point (challenge):*
  computing a correct, paired WT-vs-mutant comparison at scale (millions of
  peptide–allele pairs), plus the environment/tooling hurdles (BigMHC
  setup, per-mutation vs gene-level frequency, running the class-I pass over
  2.46 M unique 9-mers). Choose whichever challenge you want to narrate.

## 5. One biological conclusion
- **fig11_mut_vs_wt_scatter.png** — Mutant vs wild-type affinity; points below
  the diagonal are neoantigen-favourable (stronger mutant binding).
- **fig12_top_candidates.png** — Top prioritised candidates are the canonical
  colorectal driver neoantigens: KRAS G12V/G12C/G12S/G12A (largely on
  HLA-A*03:01), PIK3CA E542K, SMAD4 R361H, plus ERBB3, FBXW7, PTEN R130Q.
  *Talking point (conclusion):* a data-driven, expression- and
  recurrence-aware pipeline independently rediscovers the known colorectal
  neoantigen landscape — and KRAS G12x on HLA-A*03:01 is a documented,
  clinically pursued neoantigen.
